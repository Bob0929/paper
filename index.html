Small Trades

an idle accounting of small gains

This is a small ledger of imaginary trade — paper turned to coin, coin turned to ink, ink turned to books, each trade struck at whatever price the market happens to be offering the moment you click. Nothing sells itself; every gain here was a decision, not a background number.

Design Note

One idea from Hunicke, LeBlanc, and Zubek's MDA framework is that a game's felt experience (the "aesthetics") comes from dynamics — how mechanics actually play out at runtime — not from the mechanics themselves. A reader encounters this directly in the split within Small Trades between the Upgrades card, where paper, ink, and books are gathered automatically once purchased, and the Market card, where every trade is a manual click at a live, drifting price. My interpretation: automating the repetitive action (clicking Gather) while keeping the one meaningful choice (when to trade) manual is what should produce a feeling of ongoing agency rather than passive waiting. The open limitation is that I haven't verified this with an actual player — it's possible one manual decision point isn't enough to sustain that feeling past the first few minutes. This reasoning is what caused me to reject a flat automatic-sell rate I'd built earlier: a fixed rate would have quietly removed the only decision the player had left.

Source: Hunicke, R., LeBlanc, M., & Zubek, R. (2004). MDA: A Formal Approach to Game Design and Game Research. users.cs.northwestern.edu/~hunicke/MDA.pdf
